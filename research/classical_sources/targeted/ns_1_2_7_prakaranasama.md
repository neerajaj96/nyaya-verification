## ROOT_TEXT — Sūtra (7) (image-verified English display)

"THE NEUTRALISED PROBANS IS THAT WHICH IS PUT FORWARD TO ESTABLISH A DEFINITE
CONCLUSION, WHILE IT IS ONE THAT ONLY GIVES RISE TO SUSPENSE (AND
VACILLATION) IN REGARD TO THE POINT AT ISSUE." (Sū. 7; "the Third Fallacious
Probans" per Jha's section header — i.e. third in the 1.2.4–9 sequence.)

## BHĀṢYA [P. 53, L. 2–12] (image-verified)

- *prakaraṇam* (point at issue) = TWO OPPOSITE VIEWS on a doubtful question,
  NEITHER definitely ascertained.
- *cintā* (suspense) = the desire-to-ascertain-the-truth investigation-process
  running from doubt to definitive cognition.
- Mechanism: a probans that only feeds this suspense, put forward AS IF
  leading to definitive cognition, "does not differ (in point of being
  doubtful) from the point at issue; as both sides would be equal (equally
  doubtful)"; being SIMILAR (*sama*) to the point at issue (*prakaraṇa*), it
  yields no definite conclusion.
- Example: "Sound is non-eternal, because we do not find in it the properties
  of the eternal thing" + dish-analogy (non-possession of eternal-properties
  ⇒ non-eternal, as dishes).
- Footnote gloss: the two views are made probandum (*sādhyābhinna?* — OCR
  degraded; sense: each party's probandum) by the two parties; suspense comes
  from the real truth being unknown; the urging that proves BOTH eternal and
  non-eternal "constitutes the fallacy called prakaraṇasama"; "both sides
  would be equal" — Tātparya gloss: the term denotes "being … neutralised,
  having an opponent equally strong" (image-verified).

## VĀRTTIKA [P. 175–176] (extraction-verified, image not rendered)

- Q: whence the suspense? A: from the real truth NOT being known (known
  vaguely/generally, special features unknown).
- INCONCLUSIVE vs NEUTRALISED distinction (the load-bearing Vārttika
  contribution): the sound/eternal-properties reasoning would be
  *Inconclusive* ONLY IF the non-finding were known to subsist in a thing
  admitted-by-both-parties to be eternal (or the reverse for non-eternal);
  as neither condition holds — mere double non-finding — the two
  circumstances neutralize each other → *Neutralised*.
- Objection (foreseen): Neutralised == Unknown (asiddha), since the
  non-perceived eternal-properties are as unproved as eternality itself →
  no separate category needed.
- Reply: NOT the same. Unknown = character put forward is as unknown/to-be-
  proved AS the probandum itself; Neutralised = character put forward only
  gives rise to doubt/suspense/diversity-of-views.
