## ROOT_TEXT — the seven sūtras (Jha English display; layer-separated)

- 2.1.1 (pūrvapakṣa bundle): "No doubt can arise either from the certain
  cognition of properties common to several objects, or from the certain
  cognition of characters belonging to only one of the objects" — with three
  sub-statements (I: denies 1-1-23's first clause; II: denies its second;
  structure clarified by Bhāṣyachandra per Jha fn).
- 2.1.2: "Nor from the cognition of diversity of opinions, or from that of
  uncertainty."
- 2.1.3: "Also because in a case of diversity of opinion there is certainty
  of conviction" (two persons certain of opposite ideas, e.g. soul
  exists/does-not-exist).
- 2.1.4: "Further, because uncertainty itself is quite certain in its
  uncertain character" (if certain → not uncertainty; if not certain in
  being uncertain → not real uncertainty either).
- 2.1.5: "Lastly, doubt would never cease, inasmuch as the property (whose
  cognition gives rise to doubt) continues to exist" (tallness persists while
  pondering the post, so doubt should persist).
- 2.1.6 (siddhānta): "When doubt is held to arise ONLY from such cognition
  as has been described (in 1-1-23) as NOT APPREHENDING THE SPECIFIC
  CHARACTER of any one object — there is no possibility of either there
  being no doubt at all, or of there arising a doubt that would unceasingly
  persist."
- 2.1.7 (prayojana): "Wherever there is doubt, there is possibility of the
  aforesaid questions and answers" — i.e. the pūrvapakṣa/siddhānta Q&A
  method applies to every investigation preceded by doubt.

## BHĀṢYA (Vātsyāyana; refs [P. 60–64])

- On 2.1.1: four pūrvapakṣa readings built on senses of *upalabdhi*/
  *vyavasthā*-type terms (mere presence vs cognition; recognition in one of
  two things; definite ascertainment; restated objection). Doubt does NOT
  arise from: bare presence of common properties (recognition required);
  recognition confined to one of the two things; conviction (*adhyavasāya*
  = certain cognition — "from conviction there cannot arise doubt, which
  stands for uncertain cognition; no cause-effect affinity"); cognition of
  one thing (colour) producing doubt about another (touch); nor from the
  property of only one of two things (that yields certain conviction).
- On 2.1.2: diversity-of-opinions and uncertainty, each alone, yield no
  doubt; uncertainty cognized as "no certainty either way" is itself a
  determinate cognition excluding doubt.
- On 2.1.4: the certain/uncertain dilemma above.
- On 2.1.5: persistence objection (cause present → effect must persist).
- On 2.1.6 (reply): doubt arises from cognition-of-common-property that
  apprehends NO specific/distinctive feature; replies (a)–(d): recognition
  reading; "same thing" reading (doubt regards the SAME thing's unperceived
  distinctive features, not another thing — with Benares/Puri ms. variants);
  affinity reading (cause and effect share non-apprehension of specific
  character); persistence dissolved (doubt ceases when the distinctive
  feature IS perceived, though the common property remains).
- On 2.1.7: wherever inquiry (treatise or controversy) is preceded by doubt,
  the opponent's denial-of-doubt must be met with the above answers; hence
  doubt is examined first as pertaining to ALL investigations.

## VĀRTTIKA (Uddyotakara; refs [P. 183–185, 135])

- Introductory: why Doubt before Pramāṇas — doubt is "principal accessory
  of all examination" per nirṇaya-sūtra 1-1-41 (ascertainment after pondering
  two sides); order-change justified.
- BUT: doubt is NOT necessary for all definitive cognition — only for
  *enquiry* (vicāra): "Definitive cognition may or may not be preceded by
  doubt, but enquiry must always be preceded by doubt." Discussion/treatise
  parties (learned) may be certain; students/tutor may doubt (with Tātparya
  gloss).
- On 2.1.2: "difference of opinion AND uncertainty" readings — doubt arises
  from the cognition thereof, not from the mere facts.
- On 2.1.4: every uncertainty is certain in its own character → no real
  uncertainty (textual note: printed *vyavasthā* should read *avyavasthā*).
- On 2.1.5: persistent causes → eternal doubt (endorsing the objection
  before the 2.1.6 reply).
- On 2.1.7: disputant must not deny the doubt at issue (blocks truth-seeking);
  opponent's denial (via 1–3 arguments) is met with 2.1.6 siddhānta arguments
  — a debate-protocol instruction to the pupil.
- Rejects Bhāṣya reading (a) of 2.1.1 (Vārttika fn, L711).

## Direct textual claims

1. In NS 2.1.1–5, doubt is under pūrvapakṣa attack from five angles
   (common-property presence/recognition, one-thing cognition,
   diversity-of-opinion, uncertainty, persistence); 2.1.6 answers all via the
   non-apprehension-of-specific-character restriction (1-1-23).
2. In NS 2.1.6/Bhāṣya, doubt's cause is cognition that presents a common
   property WITHOUT the distinctive feature; doubt ceases upon perceiving
   the distinctive feature.
3. In the Vārttika, doubt is necessary for enquiry but not for definitive
   cognition; adhyavasāya (certain conviction) excludes doubt by definition
   (no cause-effect affinity between conviction and doubt).
4. In NS 2.1.7 (+Vārttika + three later notes), doubt-first Q&A is the
   universal investigation protocol; denial-of-doubt is itself a disputational
   move to be answered, not conceded.

## Interpretive claims (marked)

- The 2.1.6 restriction (viśeṣa-anupalabdhi as doubt-condition) is the
  functional ancestor of TS pakṣatā's doubt-component — but WITHOUT desire
  (siṣādhayiṣā) or proof-absence (siddhyabhāva) machinery, which are
  unattested in this section.
- Vārttika's enquiry/cognition split anticipates a two-track verifier
  architecture (enquiry-mode vs assent-mode), but that is OUR abstraction,
  not their claim.

## Computational relevance (no model built)

- Doubt-conditions are finitely listable (common-property-without-
  distinctive-feature; two-sided alternatives; neither-certain; persistent-
  cause dissolved by feature-perception) — candidate pre-gate predicates for
  D1 IF siṣādhayiṣā/siddhyabhāva gaps are filled elsewhere.
- niścaya appears ONLY as adhyavasāya (conviction) contrasted with doubt —
  no graded certainty, no threshold semantics (see D6 notes file).
- The 2.1.7 debate protocol (deny-doubt → answer-with-siddhānta) is the
  earliest sourced dialogue rule in the corpus — candidate debate-protocol
  ancestor, unimplemented.

## What this passage does NOT establish (mandatory)

- It does NOT define pakṣatā, siṣādhayiṣā, or siddhyabhāva (all absent).
- It does NOT grade uncertainty (no degrees, no probabilities, no thresholds).
- It does NOT connect doubt to vyāpti, hetu-occurrence, or concomitance.
- It does NOT license "assumed" facts as doubt-resolvers (D1's assume-hetu
  pattern has no support here).
- It does NOT settle whether doubt precedes every inference (Vārttika says
  enquiry needs doubt; definitive cognition need not follow it).
