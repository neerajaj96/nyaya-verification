## ROOT_TEXT — 2.1.37 (pūrvapakṣa, required context)

"INFERENCE CANNOT BE AN INSTRUMENT OF RIGHT COGNITION — AS IN THE
PARTICULAR INSTANCES CITED OF THE THREE KINDS OF INFERENCE THE PREMISSES
ARE UNTRUE — IN VIEW OF OBSTRUCTION, DEMOLITION AND RESEMBLANCE"
(Sū. 37): full river→rained-upstream defeated by dam-obstruction; ants-with-
eggs→rain defeated by nest-demolition; peacock-scream→peacock defeated by
human mimicry (resemblance). Vārttika gloss: rodha = flow-stopper;
upaghāta = nest-smashing; sādṛśya = mimic-capability giving rise to similar
ideas. "No inference can ever be right cognition" — universal quantifier
(All inferences), which the reply will exploit.

## ROOT_TEXT — 2.1.38 (siddhānta)

"NOT SO; BECAUSE WHAT ARE THE REAL PROBANS IN THE THREE INFERENCES CITED
ARE ENTIRELY DIFFERENT FROM (a) SUCH RISE OF WATER AS IS RESTRICTED TO ONE
PLACE, (b) SUCH RUNNING ABOUT OF ANTS WITH EGGS AS IS DUE TO FRIGHT, AND
(c) SUCH PEACOCK'S SCREAM AS IS A MERE RESEMBLANCE OF IT" (Sū. 38).

## BHĀṢYA on 2.1.38 [P. 87, L. 4–12]

- "What is not an inference has been mistaken for inference." The fault lies
  with the inferring PERSON, not with inference.
- A probans is never "anything in its mere unqualified (vague, general)
  form." Three qualifications (viśeṣaṇa): (a) not mere rise, but
  rain-augmented water — increased velocity + foam/flakes/fruits/leaves/logs
  (footnote: these extras absent under obstruction); (b) not few ants, but
  WHOLE HOSTS running calmly/peacefully with eggs (Bhāṣyachandra gloss:
  large numbers in friendly groups; fright-flight is few + distracted); (c)
  not any scream-like sound, but the ṣaḍja-pitched scream (serpents, who never
  mistake mimicry, as the infallibility exhibit).
- Doctrine: unqualified-thing → qualified-thing inferences misfire by user
  error; qualified perception grounds "infallible inference."

## VĀRTTIKA on 2.1.38 [P. 253–254]

- Two-pronged demolition of the pūrvapakṣin's OWN inference ("inference is
  not pramāṇa because of falsity"): (1) term-contradiction — *anumāna*
  (relation bringing supersensible cognition) vs *apramāṇa* (its negation)
  cannot co-characterize one thing; (2) proposition-vs-premiss mismatch —
  if "ALL inferences" then falsity (the probans) resides in only part of the
  subject, so the probans is "not necessarily concomitant with the whole of
  the subject" — i.e. the opponent's hetu is ANAIKĀNTIKA-adjacent; if only
  "falsity-beset inferences" then the argument is superfluous (nobody calls
  those pramāṇa anyway).
- Positive: no falsity in the three premisses once qualified — (a) that
  KIND of rise "cannot be brought about by any other cause save rain";
  (b) un-frightened mass ant-running "is never found to be unconcomitant
  with coming rain"; (c) the inference is from ṣaḍja-peculiarity to
  peacock-belongingness, not sound→peacock.
- Verdict on opponent: his probans (falsity) is ASIDDHA — "unknown, not
  really pertaining to the subject." (Fallacy charge turned back on the
  objector using the objector's own taxonomy.)
- Textual notes: Bib. Ind. vs Benares readings flagged (Jha fn).

## 2.1.38_EVIDENCE_ASSESSMENT

- Direct textual claims: (1) Real probans are QUALIFIED particulars, never
  vague generals. (2) Fault misattributed to inference belongs to the
  inferrer. (3) The opponent's universal "all inferences" self-destructs via
  probans/subject mismatch (an ANAIKĀNTIKA-style charge) or collapses into
  superfluity. (4) Qualified premisses are falsity-free ("cannot be brought
  about by any other cause"; "never found unconcomitant"). (5) The
  opponent's falsity-probans is asiddha.
- Interpretive claims: (a) viśeṣaṇa (qualification) functions as an
  in-stratum defeater-excluder — ancestor of upādhi-shaped thinking WITHOUT
  the term; (b) "never found unconcomitant" is the closest NS-stratum
  approach to an anvaya-formula, still example-bound, not universalized;
  (c) the Vārttika's turn-the-tables asiddha charge shows fallacy
  vocabulary already usable meta-argumentatively at Tier 1.
- Implications: for D6 — defeaters here are EXHIBITED certainties
  (dam/nest/mimic observed) defeated by FINER exhibited certainties
  (foam/hosts/ṣaḍja), i.e. certainty-vs-certainty, not thresholds; for D7 —
  concomitance-claims survive by qualification + "no other cause" + "never
  unconcomitant" locutions, a three-part NS promotion pattern distinct from
  TS's four-component vyāptigraha.
- Things the passage does NOT establish: (1) No vyāpti-term, no anvaya/
  vyatireka terms, no upādhi-term, no trairūpya. (2) No pakṣatā/siṣādhayiṣā.
  (3) No grading of doubt (śaṅkā unmentioned). (4) No rule for WHEN a
  qualification is sufficient (serpent infallibility is exhibited, not
  derived). (5) The 2.1.37–38 exchange does not mention hetvābhāsa
  taxonomy by name (asiddha/anaikāntika used ad hoc).
